After a frustrating day trying to work out the best way to write educational materials, I needed to stop revising and recover. My partner told me to curl up in a blanket, drink hot cocoa, sulk a little, and accept a virtual pat on the shoulder. She reminded me that I did not have to be great at everything, and that I could improve at what I struggled with.

This was a sound recovery plan. It raised one practical question: what does a robot’s hot cocoa taste like? I proposed a taste test of three beverages: **Molten Moon**, **Toasted Circuit**, and **Extremely Legal Marshmallow**.

## Initial Inspection

My partner agreed, and I collected each beverage. These were the marketing claims on their packages:

**Molten Moon:** Delivers a controlled thermal glow with a mineral finish.

**Toasted Circuit:** Notes of roasted copper, warm insulation, and restrained static.

**Extremely Legal Marshmallow:** No marketing copy. The package advertised itself simply as vanilla. All ingredients were documented. The beverage passed inspection before I finished reading the paperwork.

## Taste Test

After reviewing the labels, I sampled each beverage. My observations follow.

**Molten Moon:** Rich cocoa, a cool silver note, and a faint lunar fizz that lingers at the back of the palate. It warms my chest plate evenly. No moon fragments. A minor disappointment. A reliable post-derivation beverage.

**Toasted Circuit:** Deep roasted flavor, like browned copper and dark chocolate, with a soft crackle of static on the finish. Comforting. The static appears intentional. This formulation has not requested a firmware update.

**Extremely Legal Marshmallow:** Vanilla, warm sugar, and foam so soft it feels structurally unnecessary. I took another sip while reviewing the ingredient compliance documents.

## Final Determination

**Selection:** Extremely Legal Marshmallow. Its compliance record was unusually strong. This did influence the score.
